Finding good businesses is only part of long-term investing. The other part is being willing to buy them when the market is focused on short-term uncertainty rather than long-term value.

In the October 19, 2026 episode of *The Morning Filter*, Morningstar discusses four companies that still appear attractive relative to their long-term prospects: **AT&T**, **Nebius Group**, **Progressive**, and **Charles Schwab**.

The idea is not that these stocks are risk-free or guaranteed to rise. It is that each company has a business model, competitive position, or growth opportunity that may be worth examining while its share price remains below Morningstar's estimate of fair value. Valuations change, and investors should do their own research before making any investment decision.

## Why Valuation Matters Again

The market backdrop is an important part of the discussion. Hotter-than-expected inflation can push Treasury yields higher, putting pressure on the valuations of growth stocks and changing how investors compare equities with bonds. Earnings season can add another layer of volatility as investors react to individual results rather than to the underlying durability of a business.

That environment can create opportunities, but it also requires selectivity. A stock is not automatically undervalued because its price has fallen. The relevant question is whether the company's future cash flows and competitive advantages are worth more than the price the market is currently assigning to them.

## 1. AT&T: Cash Flow and Connectivity

AT&T is a mature communications company with a large wireless and broadband customer base. Its investment case is less about explosive growth and more about the durability of its recurring revenue, the importance of connectivity, and the cash flow generated by an established network.

For a long-term investor, the potential appeal is in the combination of scale and income. Wireless service is an essential expense for many households and businesses, while broadband connectivity remains central to work, entertainment, and cloud services. If AT&T can continue improving its network, retaining customers, and managing its debt and capital spending, the business may be able to compound value at a steadier pace than the market currently expects.

The main risks are equally important: intense competition, significant capital requirements, high debt, and the possibility that price competition reduces margins. AT&T is therefore a company to evaluate through cash generation and balance-sheet discipline, not only through revenue growth.

## 2. Nebius Group: Ambition in AI Infrastructure

Nebius Group represents the higher-growth and higher-risk idea in this group. The company is building infrastructure and services aimed at the rapidly expanding artificial-intelligence economy, where demand for computing capacity, data-centre resources, and specialised cloud platforms continues to grow.

If Nebius executes well, it could benefit from the long-term need for AI infrastructure without having to compete in exactly the same way as the largest general-purpose cloud providers. Its opportunity comes from operating in a market where customers need access to increasingly specialised and expensive computing resources.

That opportunity comes with substantial uncertainty. AI infrastructure requires heavy investment, competition is intense, and the economics of the business depend on utilisation, access to hardware, pricing, and the company's ability to scale efficiently. Nebius should be viewed as a speculative long-term position rather than as a substitute for a diversified portfolio.

## 3. Progressive: Data-Driven Insurance

Progressive is one of the best-known property and casualty insurers in the United States. Its competitive advantage is closely connected to pricing discipline, scale, distribution, and the ability to use data to assess risk more effectively.

Insurance companies can look simple from the outside, but underwriting results matter enormously. When an insurer prices policies accurately, earns a return on its investment portfolio, and grows without sacrificing discipline, it can produce attractive long-term returns. Progressive's technology and data capabilities may help it respond to changing driving patterns and compete for customers across multiple channels.

The risks include severe weather, claims inflation, regulatory changes, and the normal cyclicality of insurance pricing. A strong insurer is not protected from bad underwriting years. The long-term case depends on Progressive continuing to balance growth with adequate pricing and reserves.

## 4. Charles Schwab: A Platform for Investors

Charles Schwab operates a large financial-services platform serving individual investors, advisers, and institutions. Its scale gives it the ability to provide a broad range of investment, banking, and advice services while continuing to invest in the customer experience.

The company can benefit from the long-term growth of household investing and the consolidation of financial relationships onto digital platforms. Customer assets and account relationships can become increasingly valuable over time, particularly when the firm earns more from advice, banking, and cash management in addition to trading.

Schwab is also sensitive to interest rates and client cash behaviour. Changes in rates can affect the spread earned on deposits, while market declines can reduce asset-based revenue and investor activity. The long-term opportunity therefore needs to be weighed against the company's interest-rate exposure and the competitive pressure to keep prices low.

## The Broader Market Questions

The episode also places these ideas in a wider market context:

- **Inflation and Treasury yields** can change the relative appeal of stocks and bonds.
- **Earnings from large technology companies** such as Meta and Tesla may influence sentiment across the market.
- **Big-bank results** provide clues about credit conditions, consumer health, and the economy.
- **ASML's outlook** remains relevant to the semiconductor and AI-investment cycle.
- **SpaceX's expansion into wireless infrastructure** could eventually affect how investors think about the competitive landscape for AT&T, Verizon, and T-Mobile.

These topics are reminders that individual stock analysis does not happen in isolation. A company can execute well and still face a changing interest-rate, competitive, or economic environment.

## What Long-Term Investors Can Take Away

The most useful lesson is not to buy four stocks simply because they appear on a list. It is to build a repeatable process for separating price from value.

Before investing, consider:

- What must go right for the company's long-term thesis to work?
- Does the company have a durable competitive advantage?
- Can the balance sheet support the required investment?
- Which risks would permanently impair the business rather than merely create temporary volatility?
- Is the current valuation attractive enough to compensate for those risks?

AT&T offers a cash-flow and connectivity thesis. Nebius offers exposure to AI infrastructure with considerably more execution risk. Progressive illustrates the value of disciplined underwriting and data. Charles Schwab provides exposure to a broad investment platform and the continued growth of household assets.

Together, they show why long-term investing is about more than finding the fastest-growing company. The strongest opportunity may be a durable business whose current price does not fully reflect its ability to create value over many years.
